## NW-63r — bounding-box overlay + phrase-edit (must remainder)

**Scope decision: IN — as the split pre-decided it, and no wider.** ADR-029
already split NW-63: w17 shipped the viewer over real pages with text-level
`SourceSpan` highlight; **this wave is the deferred half only** — (a) `prebuilt-layout`
+ widened `words`/`polygon` wire + widened `AiOcrPage`; (b) geometry columns
(ADR-003 w17 clause 2 refused them then, owed here); (c) the phrase-edit write
path. Nothing else. **Do not pull the w17 half back** (viewer, jump-to-page,
`SourcePage`+`SourceSpan`) and do not re-litigate the split (binding instruction
§0.4; ADR-001 w17 clause 7 with its condition that the remainder heads W18 —
satisfied).

**Acceptance wording** (observable on `dev`, second-browser read-back per the
standing persistence rule):

> **N17r** — on `dev`: open a cited field on a validated Northwind PDF → the viewer
> draws a **box on the page** (not just the text-level highlight); edit an OCR
> phrase → the edit **persists** (reload **and** a second browser agree), and the
> write **keeps proposal vs override distinct** — the original proposal is never
> silently overwritten, and a human correction is what the second browser reads.

The phrase-edit provenance is the one open question this seat must settle, and it
is **OQ-w18-002** ("does the edit rewrite the text, the evidence row, or both?").
**Ruling (product half; mechanism is software-architect's):** an edit **never
rewrites the proposal in place** — it writes an **override** that lives beside the
proposal, both persisted, so provenance never blurs (`ExtractionEvidence.cs:14-19`
exists precisely for this). A correction that overwrites the extracted text is a
fabricated-fact reset of the record the whole product is built on (spec §2 "AI is
not the database"; Appendix C "never destructively overwrite … human corrections").
The proposal row and the override row are the two shapes; the screen reads the
override when present, never deletes the proposal.

**Fence carried forward, binding on the decomposer** (quoted from `w17-hitl.md`
§2): the viewer copy that shipped in w17 reads *"Page N — the wording is
highlighted below"* and **must not have promised a box** — that copy has been
fenced since w17 and is **honoured until this lands**, i.e. the w17 wording stays
until the box is real, then the copy is upgraded in the same task that draws it.

**ADR action: `amend` ADR-017** (widened wire/`AiOcrPage`, `prebuilt-layout` now
called) + **`amend` ADR-003** (geometry columns land) + **`amend` ADR-027**
(reprocess re-derives boxes). ADR-029 governs — **no new ADR**. This seat owns the
acceptance wording and the provenance ruling; the gateway model is cloud's, the
contract+migration software's, the overlay client's, the affordance ux's.

## NW-56 — Contract 360 "Ask about it" briefs that contract (should)

**Scope decision: IN.** This is the scoped-chat entry from the Contract 360
header (`Contract360Header.tsx:49` → `/ask?scope=${contractId}`). Today `AskRoute`
applies the R-ASK-10 gate first and **ignores `?scope=`**, so the operator lands on
a generic gate — the scoped entry does not brief its own contract. Spec §8.2 makes
Contract 360 the destination of citations *and* the place Ask is context-of-screen
(`percorso-pilota-v1.md` §4: "Su un contratto aperto, Ask è di quel contratto").

**Scope boundary (this seat's, against spec §1.2 and the pilot path):** this is a
**scoped chat about one contract**, not the upload onboarding and not a new chatbot.
ADR-024's "one engine, different surfaces" already covers it. **No new endpoint is
promised here** — the decision is on the *entry*: the `?scope=` handler must brief
the scoped contract (name + a contract-specific suggestion set), not land on the
generic hello/gate.

**Acceptance wording:**

> **N11** — on `dev`: from a live Contract 360, "Ask about it" opens Ask **scoped to
> that contract** — the context names the contract and the suggestion chips are
> contract-specific — and the answer is grounded in that contract's evidence; a
> `?scope=` deep-link to a contract the caller cannot see yields the ordinary
> refusal, never a leak and never a cross-tenant brief.

**ADR action: `amend` ADR-024** (scoped entry path names the `?scope=` handler
that briefs the contract) + cite ADR-012 (generated client, no new route). The
scoped-chat mechanism is software-architect's; the surface copy/IA ux's; the route
read-back client's.

## NW-57 — Quote check: market benchmark, not a manual savings worksheet (should)

**Scope decision: IN, and the job-to-be-done is fixed.** The raw item: "keep every
request; no 'send to Home'." The product promise (spec §11) is *a new proposal is
assessable in minutes* — benchmark match → market assessment → savings range → target
→ negotiation strategy — **not** a manual worksheet where the operator types their
own savings target. The design oracle (`screens-v2.md` §10) is benchmark-first with
history; today `TargetStep` types an **editable savings target** and the CTA is
"See it in Savings →".

**Rulings that bind the decomposition:**

1. **The primary output is the market benchmark assessment.** Above / in-line /
   below market, with its **adapter, sample size and as-of date** — the two honest
   shapes ADR-001 w17 clause 4 fixed (a representative position with provenance,
   or explicit "insufficient market data"). **Never a percentile alone, never
   "market" unqualified.** No paid API (NW-52 stays DEFERRED; the benchmark is the
   fixture/market-feed adapter, labelled representative).
2. **"Send to Home" / "See it in Savings" does not ship as the assessment's CTA.**
   The CTA is the **assessment + negotiation strategy** the quote *is*. "Home
   savings come demo" is out of the pilot (`percorso-pilota-v1.md:148`); the quote
   check is itself optional/non-hero (`:109`). A CTA that routes the operator away
   from the assessment they came for fails the job.
3. **Every request is kept** (the record survives; ADR-028/ADR-001 w16's read-back
   posture), and **history is the gap**: the quote check should show its own past
   requests, not push the operator to Savings.
4. **No `send to Home`**; the quote path owns its outcome. This is a product scope
   fence, not a mechanism claim.

**Acceptance wording:**

> **N12** — on `dev`: upload a supplier quote → the assessment renders a market
> position (above/in-line/below) with its provenance, or the explicit
> "insufficient market data" result; **no** manual savings-target worksheet is the
> primary output; the CTA leads to the negotiation strategy, never a "send to
> Home/Savings"; every request is retained and re-opens from the quote list.

**ADR action: `amend` ADR-024** (the quote benchmark-first flow) + cite ADR-028
(server-side state: history) + confirm ADR-001 (fixture, no paid API). Note this
item is likely the **cap overflow candidate** among Ask/Quote items, but it is
`should`, not a `must`, and the cut order is the raw file's.

## NW-59 — Ask never dead-ends (should)

**Scope decision: IN.** `ReplyBody.tsx:75` renders an abstain as `.abstain-block`
only, and `replyTypes.ts:50` states "abstain/error never carry actions" —
**contradicting R-ASK-07**, which forbids the abstain block as the *only* UX and
requires a next step. `percorso-pilota-v1.md` §3's abstain rule is "abstain" for
insufficient coverage/confidence, but nothing says *and then stop* — the pilot's own
minute 15–19 treats abstain as a live beat ("Meglio non so che inventare"), not a
dead end.

**Ruling:** every abstain/error carries a **clickable next step** — the action that
unblocks *that* failure, selected, not invented:

- insufficient coverage → **Go to Documents / Upload**;
- a field not officialized → **Review** (the recoverable path);
- off-domain / greeting → the warm **redirect** (ADR-024 already models `redirect`);
- an error turn → **Retry** plus an escape.

The action is **from the capability catalog** (ADR-024's catalog-routed hrefs only:
`/documents?…`, `/contracts/{id}`, `/ask` with follow-ups), never a fabricated
assertion. This is the product-visible companion to ADR-024's `redirect`/`refusal`
kinds, and the fence is spec §8.4's "no evidence, no claim" inverted: **no answer
may end without telling the user what to do next**, even when the answer is "I can't
reliably answer."

**Acceptance wording:**

> **N13** — on `dev`: an abstain and an error turn each render a clickable next
> step that corresponds to the failure (upload/review/redirect/retry); no abstain
> or error turn reaches a terminal block with no action; a second browser sees the
> same recovery affordance.

**ADR action: `none`** — ADR-024 (kinds `redirect`/`refusal`, catalog hrefs) +
ADR-020 (the abstain block) already own the surface; this item wires the action
on, it does not invent a new answer kind. Client/ux own the control; the action's
routing is software's.

   outranks every `should`/`could`. Its provenance fence (proposal never overwritten)
   and its read-back (second browser) are **never narrowed** — narrowing those is
   narrowing a `must`.

   recorded queue. NW-74 keeps `should` (`w17-hitl.md` §5), and this seat records
   it as **not demoted** even though it is presentation, not scope.
3. **If the cap binds, the cut order is the tail, never the head** (§0.2): NW-40/NW-41
   runbook walks first, then NW-30/NW-50 reconciliations, then the Ask/Quote
   `should`s in raw-file order. **NW-63r's durability and phrase-edit provenance are
   not a release valve.**
4. **NW-56 → NW-57 → NW-59 share the Ask surface** and, per ADR-024's one-engine rule,
   should phase-separate or co-locate under the single-writer-per-phase constraint —
   they are one surface, not three chatbots.
