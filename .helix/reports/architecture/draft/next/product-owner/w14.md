## NW-58 — Invites are email + link; login joins that workspace; Admin remove requires a new invite

### The conflict, stated exactly

| Oracle | Says |
|---|---|
| `inputs/product-spec.md` §20 Day 1 | "Create a workspace and **invite Procurement users**." |
| `inputs/product-spec.md` §16 R0 | "Auth, workspace, multi-tenancy, **roles**, upload, storage, DB, audit baseline" → "A secure workspace can ingest documents" |
| `inputs/product-spec.md` §16.1 | "Email alerts" is **P1**, not P0 |
| `inputs/product-spec.md` §13.4 | "email notifications" is **P1 / V1** |
| `inputs/product-spec.md` §1.2 | email/invitation is **not** in the non-goal list |
| `inputs/percorso-pilota-v1.md` §2 "Non è onboarting" | "**email**" |
| `inputs/percorso-pilota-v1.md` §5 (Workspace & members) | "Invite Procurement. **Niente email nel pilota.**" |
| `inputs/percorso-pilota-v1.md` §7 | "Email, mobile, Swagger, production Entra — **Non servono a mostrare il processo**", under the heading "Fuori da questo pilota **anche se restano nello spec V1 pieno**" |

The two oracles do **not** contradict each other, and the intake's framing of
OQ-w14-002 ("is email in scope?") is the wrong question. §7's own heading is
the resolution: email is *out of the pilot path*, and it *stays in the full V1
spec* — at **P1**. The pilot excludes the **transport**, three separate times.
Neither oracle excludes **inviting**; §20 requires it and §5 keeps "Invite
Procurement" on the pilot's own Workspace & members screen.

So the split is not "email yes / email no". It is **transport vs lifecycle**.

### Scope decision

| Sub-capability | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Invitation entity: token, role, expiry, single use | **IN** | Without it "invite" grants nothing an invitee can act on. §16 R0 "roles"; §20 Day 1. |
| Accept → sign in → **join that workspace** | **IN** | NW-58 must #2. This is the whole delta: today the invitee is never told and cannot reach the tenant. |
| Admin **remove** a member; never the last Admin | **IN** | NW-58 must #3; §3.1 "Workspace Admin — workspace config, **users, roles**". |
| Re-invite after removal | **IN** | NW-58 must #4; today a repeat invite at the same role fails outright (`WorkspaceMembershipService.cs:90-96`). |
| `IInvitationMailer` seam | **IN** | Keeps the transport a later drop-in, not a re-architecture. |
| **A real mail transport** (Azure resource + Terraform module + per-env secret) | **OUT of w14** | §16.1 and §13.4 rank email **P1**; the pilot excludes it three times. Not a §1.2 non-goal — deferred, not cancelled. |
| Copyable accept link in the Members UI | **IN**, replaces the transport for w14 | The Admin delivers the link out of band. This is exactly the pilot's "Invite Procurement / niente email". |
| "Invitation sent." copy | **OUT until a transport exists** | NW-58's own must #1: "UI must not say 'sent' unless the mail left." This is a truthfulness rule, not a scope preference — it stands even after the transport lands. |
| Invite email **must match the workspace domain** (screens-v2 §10) | **OUT of w14 → W15** | See below. |

**Why the transport is deferred, in one sentence I will defend at the table:**
w14 has four `must` items (NW-02, NW-01, NW-14, NW-58) against a 20-task cap,
and spending that cap on a **P1** capability the pilot script never shows —
a new Azure resource under a locked "cheapest tier, nothing idle-expensive"
rule, its Terraform module, its per-env secret, its promotion path — would
postpone `must` work behind a `should`. That is the trade I am chartered to
refuse. Removing the transport is what makes NW-58's four musts affordable in
this wave.

**Why the domain-match rule is deferred.** It is not in NW-58, not in §20, and
not in §3.1. It is prototype helper text. Enforcing it in w14 would break this
wave's own acceptance: **N3** is verified on `dev` by inviting a *second Entra
account*, which in practice is on a different domain than the creator's. A
validation rule that makes the wave's acceptance unverifiable is a bad trade
in the wave that introduces it. It moves to W15 with NW-05, where the domain
is a real token claim rather than a string the SPA supplies. `screens-v2` §10's
sentence stays as **helper text**, not as a server-side block.

**Placement, not a gate.** `percorso-pilota-v1.md` §2 step 2 says "Nessun invite
in hero"; step 4 says "Dopo il primo documento `completed`, **o dal footer**".
The "o dal footer" is decisive: this is a **prominence** rule, not a hard gate.
Ruling: the invite affordance must not appear as a first-run CTA in the empty
state; `/workspace/members` stays reachable at any time. No task should
implement a "you may not invite until a contract is validated" block. The
screens-v2 §10 tip ("invite the team once the first contract is validated —
there is nothing for them to ask before that") is the correct expression of it.

### Acceptance — N3b as it must be worded for w14

Replaces the intake's N3b. Every clause is observable on `dev` by a human with
two Entra accounts and two browsers. Storage shape is deliberately unspecified —
that is the architects' call; these are the product-visible facts.

**N3b-1 — the invite produces something the invitee can act on.**
Admin opens `/workspace/members`, invites the second account's address as
Procurement → the response carries a single-use accept link naming this
workspace; the UI renders that link, copyable. The UI does **not** say
"Invitation sent." and does **not** imply a mail left the system.

**N3b-2 — the roster is a server fact, and it distinguishes the two states.**
The Members table shows that address with role `Procurement` and status
`Invited`, after a full reload and from a second browser (this is NW-04's
read; N3b only asserts the two status values are distinguishable).

**N3b-3 — an unaccepted invitation grants nothing.**
Before the link is opened, signing in as the second account and listing
workspaces returns **no** workspace for that account. `Invited` ≠ member.

**N3b-4 — accepting joins *that* workspace.**
The second account opens the link, signs in with Entra, and lands **in that
workspace** — not on "create a workspace", not on a second empty tenant. The
Members row flips `Invited` → `Active`. The link does not work a second time.

**N3b-5 — removal is immediate and total.**
Admin removes that member → on the removed account's very next request the
workspace is gone from its list and any cached tenant id is refused. Not at
token expiry; on the next request.

**N3b-6 — re-adding requires a new invite.**
The removed address cannot rejoin by signing in again. A **new** invite at the
**same** role must be issuable (today this errors, `WorkspaceMembershipService.cs:95`)
and must go through N3b-3 → N3b-4 again.

**N3b-7 — the last Admin cannot be removed.**
An Admin removing the only remaining Admin is refused with a message that says
why. Enforced server-side, not by hiding the button.

**N3b-8 — the Admin gate is server-side.**
A Procurement member cannot invite or remove, and cannot obtain an accept link,
even with a hand-crafted request. The check reads the membership row, never a
client-supplied role header.

## NW-24 — Workspace has no currency / region

### What the oracles actually ask for

`percorso-pilota-v1.md` §2 step 1: *"form nome / **industria** / **paese**"* —
name, industry, country. The prototype agrees and is more specific than the
intake assumed: `markup.html:55-56` is a **Industry** closed list
(Food & beverage · Manufacturing · Financial services · Software · Other) and a
**Country** closed list (Switzerland · Italy · Germany · Austria). `markup.html:57`
is the submit button. **Currency is never asked** — it appears only in the pick
row output, `markup.html:63`: `"{{ seededCount }} validated contracts · CHF · eu-west"`.

This contradicts the intake's assumption in OQ-w14-004 ("`country` and
`currency` are added as first-class columns … `industry` is optional free
text"). The prototype asks for industry and country, both as enums, and derives
currency.

### Scope decision — **reduced**

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| `name` | already exists — unchanged |
| `industry` | **IN**, closed list, exactly `markup.html:55`'s five values. Not free text. |
| `country` | **IN**, closed list, exactly `markup.html:56`'s four values for the pilot; extending the list is a data change, not a decision. |
| `currency` | **IN as stored, OUT as a form field** — derived from `country` at create time and persisted so it is stable if the mapping later changes. The user is never asked. |
| business `region` (`eu-west`) | **IN as a derived display token**, same rule as currency. It is a *business* region and does not touch ADR-006 (`northeurope` stays). |
| Anything else on the workspace | **OUT** — no size, no sector taxonomy, no fiscal year. Not asked by any oracle. |

Rationale: the create form is the **first screen of the demo**
(`percorso-pilota-v1.md` §6, minute 0–2). Every field on it is a question asked
before the customer has seen any value. Three fields is what the pilot scripted;
a fourth (currency) that the prototype itself does not ask is scope creep on the
most expensive screen in the product.

### The rule that protects the numbers

**A workspace currency is a display default. It never overrides a contract's own
stored currency.** Savings, quote and portfolio figures are currency-tagged per
contract today, from extracted facts. A workspace-level currency that rewrote
them would corrupt validated facts and violate §2's "AI is not the database /
canonical facts live in structured storage". No task may convert, re-tag or
re-render a contract's amount using the workspace currency. If the table wants
cross-currency normalisation, that is a new item in a later wave with an FX
source, and it is not NW-24.

### Acceptance — W14-A2 as it must be worded

**W14-A2-1** — Create a workspace: the form asks **name, industry, country**
and nothing else; industry and country are closed lists matching
`markup.html:55-56`.
**W14-A2-2** — The picker row reads `"{n} validated contracts · {currency} · {region}"`
with currency and region derived from the chosen country, never typed.
**W14-A2-3** — The values survive a reload **and a second browser** — i.e. they
come from `GET /api/workspaces`, not from `localStorage`.
**W14-A2-4** — **The guard**: a contract whose extracted currency is EUR still
displays EUR in Portfolio, Contract 360 and Savings after the workspace was
created with `country = Switzerland`. Nothing is converted.

### Constraint this places on NW-09 (which does not name this seat)

If NW-24 is cut for budget, **NW-09 must not print a literal `CHF · eu-west`**
to match the prototype pixel. The row degrades to `"{n} validated contracts"`.
Shipping a hardcoded currency and region is the same class of defect as
"Invitation sent." with no mail: the screen asserting a fact the system does not
hold. This is an acceptance constraint on NW-09, for its seats to implement.

Second, the **label**. The prototype says "**validated** contracts", not
"documents". Product ruling on the wording: the number counts contracts whose
facts are canonical — the `completed` state of `percorso-pilota-v1.md` §3
("`completed` — fatti canonicali", "Ask si accende") — and the same number must
appear in the picker, the rail and `/documents`. The *query* that produces it is
software-architect's; the *label and the requirement that all three agree* is
this seat's. If the three cannot agree in this wave, change the label, not the
number.

### Priority I defend

**`should`, lowest in the wave, and the first thing to cut.** It closes no §20
sentence and no `must`. The raw file marks it `(HITL)`. I am on record: if the
decomposer approaches the 20-task cap, cut NW-24 whole (both columns and the
form change) rather than descoping NW-58 or NW-03 — with the NW-09 degradation
above applied.
